The guard law (include/pigen/pipeline_lower.h step (c), how/to/reset/and/enable/a/pipeline.md) is unambiguous and normative: when the enclosing procedural guard is false the pipeline holds every stage's storage AND disables both the input and the output handshake, so no item is accepted or emitted that cycle; "the pipeline's ready/valid equations are ALL gated by that guard." A phase-local stand-in that gates only the ready equation's value is not an acceptable narrowing: the law says all equations are gated, and a false guard must hold storage and disable both handshakes in effect, not merely in the ready equation's expression value.

The step-(c) implementation (src/pipeline_lower.c) enforces the law in the published model. Under a real (source-spelled) enclosing procedural guard: (1) each stage's next-value equation is the storage-hold mux — a PIGEN_RTL_EXPR_CONDITIONAL over the complete guard with the computed next value as when-true and one fresh synthetic per-boundary stored-value VARIABLE of the value's own type as when-false, so a false guard holds every stage's storage; (2) each stage-boundary buf instance publishes its guard connection (a 1-bit VARIABLE whose origin spells the complete guard), so the storage's guard port is wired; (3) each yield member of the outgoing-packet concatenation carries its own hold mux, so the output handshake is disabled in effect on a false guard — the concatenation itself stays the yield write's value, never wrapped in an outer conditional; (4) the ready equation remains `ready = guard`, the input-handshake half. An unguarded pipeline (the constant-1 stand-in, or a condition that cannot be lowered) publishes no guard object, no hold mux and no guard connection — the no-over-gating contract.

The enforcement is pinned at the pipeline-lower-test seam: the six ready-value sections of what/are/the/pipeline/halt/stop/test/scenarios.md (the ready equation's value) and the eight storage-hold/I/O-handshake sections of what/are/the/pipeline/guard/storage/io/test/scenarios.md (per-stage next value holds on a false guard; the hold mux's when-false stored-value object; the buf guard port; the storage false-guard hold; the input-handshake-disabled conjunction; the output-handshake gating; the unguarded-not-over-gated pin; and the terminal-SV rendering of the hold mux and the yield write), all green on master. The six ready-value sections alone cannot discharge the law: a lowering that publishes a standalone `ready = guard` on a dangling object, leaves the stage storage and the yield write ungated, and never wires the guard to storage passes all six; the eight storage-hold/I/O-handshake sections close that gap.

Remaining within the halting/stop obligation: the reset-binding coverage (pipe_reset/pipeline_reset resetting the generated valid bits) is its own later scoped mapping.
